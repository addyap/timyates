Asset-backed mortgages: borrowing against your investments

By Tim Yates · 5 min read

Some buyers have plenty of wealth but don't fit a standard mortgage. Perhaps most of their money is invested rather than earned as salary, or they're retired, or the purchase is large. Selling investments to buy outright isn't always wise either: it can trigger tax, and it takes money out of the market. An asset-backed mortgage, sometimes called a back-to-back or Lombard loan, offers another route.

How it works

You place an investment portfolio with a lender, usually a private bank, and pledge it as security. The bank lends against it, typically a proportion of its value that depends on how cautious or adventurous the investments are. The money is used to buy your property, and your portfolio stays invested, working for you, while the loan is in place.

These loans are often interest-only, with the capital repaid when the property is sold or from the portfolio later. Some lenders also take a charge over the property itself.

Who it can suit

  • Buyers with significant investments whose income doesn't meet a French bank's affordability rules.
  • Retired buyers, for whom standard terms can be short or unavailable.
  • Larger purchases, where these loans are most common.
  • People who expect their investments to grow by more than the cost of borrowing, and are comfortable with that being uncertain.

The risks to weigh

The structure only works if you're clear about the downside.

  • Markets fall. If your portfolio drops in value, the lender can ask you to add more security or repay part of the loan, sometimes at the worst moment to sell.
  • Currency. If the loan is in euros and the portfolio in sterling or dollars, exchange-rate moves can work against you.
  • Interest rates. Many of these loans have variable rates.
  • Investment returns aren't guaranteed to beat the cost of the loan.
Done well, an asset-backed loan keeps your wealth working. Done carelessly, it doubles your exposure to a bad year.

Getting it right

The lending and the investment strategy have to be designed together: how much to borrow against what, in which currency, and with what margin of safety. That joined-up view is exactly why I advise on both. If you're considering a purchase this way, let's look at the numbers before you commit.
