Moving to France: a money checklist for before and after you arrive

By Tim Yates · 7 min read

Most people plan the removal van, the schools and the healthcare carefully, then leave their money exactly as it was in the UK. That's understandable, and often expensive. Some of the most useful planning can only be done before you become French resident.

When do you become tax resident?

Broadly, France treats you as tax resident if any one of these applies: your home or main place of stay is in France, your main work is in France, or the centre of your economic interests is here. Spending more than 183 days a year in France is a common trigger, but it isn't the only one. Once you're resident, France generally taxes your worldwide income and gains, with the UK–France tax treaty deciding which country taxes what.

Before you move

  • Review your ISAs. ISAs aren't tax-free in France. The income and gains become taxable here, and they're awkward to report. Consider what to keep, and whether to take gains before you move.
  • Look at gains you're sitting on. Depending on your situation, it may make sense to realise some gains while still UK resident.
  • Think about where your savings should live. Investments that work well in the UK can be inefficient here. A French-compliant wrapper such as assurance vie is often better.
  • Understand your pensions. Most UK pensions, including the state pension, are taxed in France once you live here. Government service pensions, such as those of civil servants, teachers or the armed forces, generally remain taxed in the UK only.
  • Plan your currency transfers. A specialist currency provider will usually give you a much better rate than your bank for moving large sums.

After you arrive

  • File your first French tax return in the spring after your first year of residence, even if you think you owe nothing.
  • Declare foreign bank accounts. Every account you hold outside France, including UK current accounts, must be declared each year. The penalties for forgetting are per account.
  • Join the health system. UK state pensioners can usually register using an S1 form. Others normally join after three months of stable residence. Many people add a top-up policy.
  • Revisit your will. French inheritance rules are very different from English ones, and a UK will may not do what you expect. Talk to a notaire.
  • Check your property values. France's wealth tax now applies only to property, and only above a high threshold, but it's worth knowing where you stand.

Do it in the right order

The timing of your move, the order in which you sell or reorganise things, and which year you're resident in can all change the tax you pay. If you're planning a move to the south of France, talk to me early. A conversation six months before you arrive is worth far more than one six months after.
