What people ask me

Short answers to common questions about investing, borrowing and living in France. If yours isn't here, just ask.

Savings and investments

What is assurance vie?

A French savings and investment wrapper, not life cover in the British sense. Growth isn't taxed while it stays in the contract, withdrawals after eight years get a yearly allowance, and it can pass to your chosen beneficiaries with generous inheritance tax treatment. Read the full guide.

Are my ISAs still tax-free if I live in France?

No. France doesn't recognise ISAs, so once you're French resident the income and gains are taxable here and need to be declared. It's worth reviewing them before you move.

Can I keep my savings in sterling?

Yes. Some international assurance vie contracts let you hold investments in sterling, euros or other currencies, which can help if you have commitments or family in the UK.

Is my money safe?

All investment carries risk, and the value of investments can fall as well as rise. What I can do is make sure the risk you take is the risk you intend, and that your money is held with well-regulated providers. Contracts with Luxembourg insurers, for example, come with strong legal protection for policyholders.

Mortgages

Can I get a French mortgage if I don't live in France?

Yes. French lenders do lend to non-residents, though they often ask for a larger deposit and look closely at your income and savings. How the process works.

How much can I borrow?

French lenders generally cap total loan repayments at 35% of your income, including the new mortgage and its insurance, with terms of up to 25 years in most cases. Your deposit, savings and the type of income you have all matter too.

When should I speak to you?

Before you start viewing. Once you sign the preliminary contract, the time allowed to secure your mortgage is limited, so knowing your budget and your lender in advance makes everything calmer.

What is an asset-backed mortgage?

A loan secured against an investment portfolio rather than, or as well as, your income, so your investments stay invested. It can suit wealthier buyers, retirees and larger purchases, but it carries real risks if markets fall. Read more.

Do I need mortgage insurance?

In practice, yes: French lenders expect the loan to be covered. You're free to choose a policy other than the bank's own, which often costs less.

UK pensions in France

How are UK pensions taxed if I live in France?

Most UK pensions, including the state pension and private and workplace pensions, are taxed in France once you're resident here. Government service pensions, for former civil servants, teachers, NHS staff, police and armed forces, for example, generally remain taxable only in the UK.

Is my 25% tax-free lump sum still tax-free?

Not automatically. France doesn't recognise the UK tax-free lump sum, though some pension lump sums can qualify for a favourable flat rate here if the conditions are met. The timing of when you draw it matters, so get advice before you do.

Should I transfer my UK pension to a QROPS?

For most people living in France, probably not any more. Since October 2024, most transfers to overseas pension schemes carry a 25% UK tax charge. There are usually better ways to make your UK pensions work well while you live here.

Insurance and protection

Do I need private health insurance in France?

Once you're in the French health system, it pays a large share of most costs, and many people add a top-up policy for the rest. Before you qualify, or if you want wider cover, private medical insurance fills the gap. With colleagues across Spectrum, I can help you find the right cover.

Can you help with life insurance?

Yes, alongside the rest of your planning, whether it's cover to protect your family or the borrower insurance a French lender will expect on your mortgage.
